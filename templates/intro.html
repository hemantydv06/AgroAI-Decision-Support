Autonomous Indoor Farming
Decision Support System

A deep learning approach to agricultural telemetry processing, crop yield optimization, and live market integration.

Read Whitepaper

1. Abstract & Intro

This project introduces a state-of-the-art machine learning architecture designed to autonomously recommend optimal crops for indoor farming environments. By analyzing real-time data inputs such as Nitrogen, Phosphorus, Potassium, temperature, humidity, and soil pH, the system achieves a highly accurate classification across 19 distinct organic crops.

Precision agriculture is rapidly evolving, shifting from traditional intuition-based farming to data-driven, highly controlled environments. This system acts as an autonomous neural bridge, processing local environmental contexts to maximize both biological yield and economic return.

4. Problem Statement

How can we accurately predict the most biologically viable and economically profitable crop out of 19 variations, given a constantly shifting vector of 7 environmental variables in an indoor farming setup?

5. Methodology

The solution utilizes a Keras/TensorFlow Sequential Neural Network. The architecture employs Dense layers for initial abstraction, followed by Batch Normalization to ensure mathematical stability during gradient descent. Dropout layers are strategically placed to heavily penalize overfitting. The final layer utilizes a softmax activation function to output a precise probability distribution.

6. Dataset and Features

The model was trained on a heavily vetted agricultural dataset, subsequently cleaned to remove massive-scale exterior crops (like wheat and maize) to specialize purely on high-value, viable indoor variants (e.g., Coffee, Pomegranate, Kidney Beans).

Features underwent rigorous preprocessing using `StandardScaler` to normalize distributions, and a custom engineered feature—`NPK_sum`—was introduced to provide the network with a synthesized view of total soil nutrient density.

7. Results & Conclusion

The deployment of a Deep Learning ANN for indoor farming telemetry proves highly effective. The model converged successfully within 100 epochs, utilizing an early stopping callback to restore best weights. Evaluation on the blind test split yielded a categorical accuracy exceeding baseline expectations.
